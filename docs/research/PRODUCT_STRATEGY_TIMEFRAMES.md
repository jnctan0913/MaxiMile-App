# Product Strategy Timeframes: Singapore & SEA Fintech Benchmarks

**Date**: 2026-03-08
**Purpose**: Inform MaxiMile's strategic horizon choice with empirical data from SG/SEA fintech

---

## 1. Singapore Fintech Product Cycle Benchmarks

### Company Timelines: Founded → Launch → PMF → Revenue → Scale

| Company | Founded | Launch | Time to Launch | Key PMF Signal | Time to 100K Users |
|---------|---------|--------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **HeyMax** | Early 2023 | Sep 2023 | ~6 months | US$6M ARR (May 2025) | ~18 months (150K by Jan 2026) |
| **StashAway** | Sep 2016 | Jul 2017 | 10 months | S$20M AUM (end 2017) | ~3 years |
| **Endowus** | 2017 | Oct 2019 | ~2 years | S$1B AUM in 20 months | ~20 months post-launch |
| **Syfe** | 2017 | Jul 2019 | ~2 years | Series B 9 months after Series A | ~2.5 years |
| **Aspire** | Jan 2018 | 2018 | ~6 months | Profitability Q2 2023 | N/A (B2B) |
| **Funding Societies** | 2015 | Jun 2015 | Immediate | S$7.8M loans in 12 months | N/A (B2B) |
| **CardUp** | 2015 | 2016 | ~12 months | SGD 3.35M revenue (2020) | Acquired 2022 |

### Key Patterns
- **MVP to launch**: 6-24 months (shorter for non-regulated, longer for MAS-licensed products)
- **Launch to meaningful revenue**: 8-20 months
- **Launch to 100K users (consumer)**: 18-30 months in Singapore
- **Seed to Series A**: 14-18 months (HeyMax: 18 months, Syfe: 14 months)

## 3. Timeframe Comparison: Risks & Tradeoffs

### 6-Month Horizon — Too Short

| Concern | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Insufficient for trust-building** | Miles community is tight-knit and sceptical. MileLion endorsement requires months of proven accuracy |
| **No time for data moat** | Recommendation engine improves with transaction volume — 6 months yields insufficient data |
| **Investor misalignment** | SG VCs expect 12-18 months of planned milestones at seed stage |
| **Revenue premature** | Partners (SingSaver, banks) won't sign affiliate deals with <5K users; need user base first |
| **Regulatory incompatibility** | MAS approval alone takes 4-12 months (if needed for future payment features) |

**When it works**: Pre-launch MVP validation sprints only.

### 12-18 Month Horizon — Sweet Spot

| Why this works | Evidence |
|----------------|----------|
| **Matches investor runway** | Seed funding structured for 12-24 months; Series A investors expect 18 months of milestones |
| **Enough for PMF validation** | HeyMax proved PMF in ~18 months; Syfe in 14 months; Endowus in 20 months |
| **Fits SG VC expectations** | Antler, Iterative, January Capital, Peak XV all structured around this interval |
| **Compatible with agile execution** | 6 quarterly OKR cycles within the strategic horizon |
| **Time for data moat** | 12-18 months of transaction data = meaningful recommendation improvement |
| **Community trust cycle** | Enough time to earn MileLion endorsement, build 10K+ user base, prove accuracy |

### 24+ Month Horizon — Too Long

| Concern | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Market conditions shift** | SG Q3 2024 fintech funding dropped 51% YoY — 2-year plans built on wrong assumptions |
| **Feature obsolescence** | HeyMax could close the all-network gap within 12 months |
| **False precision** | Teams execute against the plan rather than customer signals |
| **Reduced optionality** | Locked commitments limit ability to pivot |

**When it works**: Infrastructure decisions, multi-market regulatory sequencing, talent pipeline only.

## 5. Agile Planning Hierarchy Used by SG Startups

| Level | Timeframe | Purpose |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Sprint | 2 weeks | Feature delivery and validated learning |
| OKR cycle | 90 days | Goal validation, pivot/persevere decisions |
| Product roadmap | 6-12 months | Customer and partner-facing commitments |
| Strategic roadmap | 12-18 months | Investor-facing; hiring and regulatory sequencing |
| Vision | 3 years | Market positioning; pitch narrative |

**Best practice**: Use quarterly OKRs for execution within an 18-month strategic frame. The strategy provides direction; the OKRs provide agility.

---

## 6. Implications for MaxiMile

### Why 18 Months (Not Shorter)
1. **Trust can't be sprinted** — miles community endorsement takes 6+ months of proven accuracy
2. **Data moat needs volume** — recommendation quality improves with 12+ months of transaction data
3. **Revenue needs user base** — affiliate partners require 5-10K users before signing deals
4. **HeyMax benchmark** — took 18 months from seed to Series A milestones; MaxiMile needs comparable time to build competitive position

### Why 18 Months (Not Longer)
1. **HeyMax is closing the gap** — Visa-only today, but US$11M Series A funds Mastercard/Amex expansion
2. **Market window is finite** — first-mover advantage erodes with every month HeyMax and dtenAI iterate
3. **Agile execution preferred** — quarterly OKRs within the 18-month frame allow course correction
4. **SG TAM ceiling** — ~400K addressable users means you hit diminishing returns quickly; must prove model before expanding

### Concerns About 18-Month Timeframe
1. **Features are already built** — if product is ready, why not compress to 12 months?
2. **Bootstrapped vs funded** — without VC pressure, is 18 months artificially aligned to funding cycles?
3. **HeyMax's pace** — they moved from 0 to US$6M ARR in 20 months with US$13.6M funding; can MaxiMile match without funding?
4. **Over-planning risk** — 18 months of detailed roadmap may create rigidity if market shifts
